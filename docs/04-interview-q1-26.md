## The spine (named at Q16)

International relations analyst (research papers on China) → marketing manager at 19 → tried everything (paid/earned media, social, PR, events, general marketing) → product management → now learning engineering (started ~8 months ago with vibe coding, currently taking a programming course). 4 years in Web3 (since 2022), pivoted into AI, likes combining both, still curious about traditional industries. MBA in Spain, youngest in the cohort, transitioning from executor to strategic thinker.

**The throughline: the border-crosser — she keeps crossing professional borders nobody told her she was allowed to cross.** This is the personal-brand spine.

## Beliefs & boundaries

- **Anti-FOMO selectivity** (Q1): you don't need every new model, agent, or trend. Build the best setup for yourself and test wisely. Information inflation is the big unspoken problem in Web3/AI. Be selective about what you consume.
- **AI boundary rule** (Q3–4): AI never thinks *instead of* her. Never: painting, music choices, book choices, identity/emotional decisions, choosing partners/work. Always, guilt-free: manual time-consuming work, visualizing work things (websites, landing pages). The brand document is "a writing instrument, not a replacement brain" — AI captures style and helps externalize; decisions and consequences stay hers.
- **Dignity over effectiveness** (Q13, 25–26): hates engagement-bait mechanics ("comment X and I'll DM you", "this guide will 10x your mindshare") even while admitting they work. Won't name-and-shame anyone, including grifters and rug-pullers: "I don't need to waste time on this" — economy of attention, not timidity. ⚠️ Q26 (is this value or fear?) was asked but NOT answered — voice gave out. Revisit.

## How she thinks

- **Loops, not lines** (Q6–7): circles back, self-corrects mid-stream, contradicts then resolves. Contradictions are part of the thinking process, not errors.
- **Intellectual honesty** (Q20): revises positions in real time (walked back the Abramović dismissal). Would rather say "I don't understand this yet" than fake a confident dismissal. Trusts meaning she can see the craft behind.
- **Signature argument move** (Q8): "I believe X can do a lot, but it can't do Y" — concede the capability, then name the limit. Appeared multiple times unprompted.

## The two registers (Q7–10)

1. **Analytic** (school/MBA/professional): diplomat structure — thesis intro → arguments both ways → resolution. Stays mostly neutral but always plants a flag ("I do believe…"). Raised on IR analytical reports.
2. **Emotional/personal** (friends, speaker notes): detailed, lots of examples, talks feelings directly.

**Personal-brand voice = the hard blend: simple words on the surface, deep vocabulary underneath.** "I want to be simple, but show I'm not talking like everyone else." Grammatically flawless. Good flow. Register test (Q11): chose C's honesty/rhythm + a couple of B's precise technical words → **"plain sentence, one expensive word."**

- **Speaks plain, writes rich** (Q21) — the page is where the literary register comes out (reverse of most people).
- **North star: Fei-Fei Li, "The Worlds I See"** (Q9) — scholar braided with memoirist.

## Mechanics

- **Em-dash: hard ban** (Q21). Lifelong, almost physical aversion ("panic since I was a kid"). Doubles as anti-AI-tell. Semicolons, colons, commas all welcome.
- **Closings** (Q22–23): never the thesis hammer / forceful restate. Close by **widening shot** or **open question**. "Strong close" = the last thought is the one the reader carries out the door (IR-paper bottom line), weighted, never a fade-out.
- **Openings** (Q14–15): "Unpopular opinion:" = crime. The templated humble-anecdote ("a founder asked me… so I sat with it") = suspicious. Won't dress a simple fact in fake drama. ⚠️ **Her own opening move is an open gap — she said "I don't know yet."** Revisit in Q27+.
